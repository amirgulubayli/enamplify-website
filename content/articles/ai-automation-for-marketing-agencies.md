Most conversations about AI in marketing agencies start with content generation. It is the obvious place to look, and it is usually the wrong place to start.

The biggest drain on an agency’s time is rarely writing. It is everything around the writing: turning briefs into tasks, reformatting work for each channel, chasing approvals and assembling the same client reports every month. That is where AI automation pays back first.

## Start with the work between the work

Ask your account and creative teams where their week goes. The answers tend to cluster around a few recurring jobs:

- Reading a client brief and turning it into tasks, owners and deadlines.
- Adapting one approved piece of content into versions for every channel.
- Moving drafts between reviewers and reminding people who have not replied.
- Pulling numbers from several platforms into a monthly report.

None of these needs creative judgement. All of them come before the work that does. When we built [internal workflows for a marketing agency](/work/marketing-agency-workflows/), these were the pipelines that mattered: brief intake, channel adaptation, approval routing and reporting.

## Keep the brand in the loop

Agencies are right to worry about quality. A pipeline that produces off-brand copy at speed is worse than no pipeline.

The answer is to put the brand guidance into the workflow and a person at the end of it. Content is adapted against written rules for tone, terminology and format, then queued for someone to check. The system does the repetitive transformation. The team keeps the decisions.

## Automate the handoffs before the ideas

A useful test for any agency workflow is to ask what a mistake would cost. A late reminder costs little. A misjudged campaign idea costs a client. Automate the low-cost, high-frequency steps first, and let the team’s confidence grow with the evidence.

## Make it yours

The agencies that get lasting value are the ones whose own people understand the pipelines well enough to change them. Clients change, channels change and reporting requirements change. A workflow nobody in the building can adjust becomes a new bottleneck.
