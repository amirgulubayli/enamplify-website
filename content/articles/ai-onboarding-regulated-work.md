In regulated work, a new colleague is a risk until they are trained. Every step has to be done correctly and on the record, and the procedures that describe those steps are long, detailed and easy to misread.

That makes onboarding slow and expensive. It also makes it one of the most useful places to apply AI, provided it is done carefully.

## The problem with manuals

Most regulated organisations train people with documents: standard operating procedures, work instructions and training decks. New staff read them, shadow an experienced colleague and then try the work.

The knowledge is there, but it is separated from the moment it is needed. So new people ask the same questions, experienced people answer them again, and small errors creep in between the manual and the task.

## Put the guidance inside the workflow

When we built [clinical trial management workflows](/work/clinical-trial-operations/), the aim was not only to automate steps. It was to make the correct way of doing each task the easiest way.

That meant three things:

1. **Automate the routine steps**, and log every action so the record is complete by default.
2. **Guide people through each procedure in order**, with the right information in front of them at each stage.
3. **Let people ask questions of the organisation’s own documents**, and always show the source of the answer, so nobody has to trust an unexplained reply.

The result is that a new user learns the work inside the system that does the work. Training and onboarding become much simpler, and experienced staff spend less time repeating themselves.

## Grounded, reviewed and visible

Regulated teams are right to be cautious about AI. Answers must come from approved material, not the open internet. Access must follow roles. Changes to a workflow must be reviewed before they go live.

These are not obstacles to adoption. They are the design brief. An AI workflow that cannot show its source, its owner and its history does not belong in regulated work.

## Your team, not a black box

The organisations that benefit most are the ones whose own people understand how their workflows are built, so they can update them when procedures change. That is the difference between enablement and a system that nobody inside the building can maintain.
