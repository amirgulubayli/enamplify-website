Public relations is a relationship business. That is exactly why AI in PR agencies needs a clear boundary: automate the research, prepare the pitch, and never let a system speak to a journalist on your behalf.

Drawn that way, the opportunity is large.

## Where a PR team’s time actually goes

Ask a PR account team what fills their week and the answer is rarely “pitching”. It is the preparation around it:

- Keeping track of what each journalist and outlet is covering right now.
- Building and cleaning media lists for every new story.
- Tailoring the same announcement to many different angles.
- Collecting coverage and turning it into a client report.

This work is repetitive, but it is not trivial. A pitch built on an out-of-date list damages relationships that took years to build. That is what makes it a good fit for careful automation.

## What a well-built PR workflow does

When we built [workflow automation for a PR agency](/work/pr-agency-workflows/), the design followed the shape of the job.

Media research runs continuously, so the agency’s knowledge of who covers what stays current. For each story, the system proposes a shortlist of relevant journalists and explains why each one is on it. It drafts tailored angles for a person to refine. After launch, it gathers coverage and assembles the report, so the account team can spend their time on the interpretation clients actually value.

## The rule that matters

Nothing reaches a journalist without a named person approving it. That rule is not a limitation on the system. It is what makes the system safe to use at all.

AI is good at reading widely, remembering details and producing a strong first draft. People are good at judgement, timing and trust. A PR workflow that respects that division gives the team more time for the part of the job only they can do.
